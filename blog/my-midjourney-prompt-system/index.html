My Midjourney prompt system — from v1 ugly to v5 polished

8 March 2026 5 min read
AI Midjourney Process

Most people treat AI image generation like a slot machine: type something, hit generate, hope for the best. I treat it like a design tool with a very particular interface. The difference between a mediocre output and a polished one is almost always in the prompt structure — and the structure is something you can learn, iterate on, and systematise.

Here's my five-version iteration process, shown with a real example: a hero image for this portfolio's AI Lab section.

Version 1 — The brain dump

v1 — Brain dump

abstract digital art with purple colors and geometric shapes, modern design, portfolio website hero image, clean aesthetic

Result: Generic, oversaturated, looks like every other AI image. No composition control. The model fills in everything and nothing feels intentional.

Version 1 is always terrible. It's the equivalent of asking a designer to "make it look nice" with no brief. The model has too much freedom and defaults to its training data averages — which means bland.

Version 2 — Adding structure

v2 — Adding structure

hero banner composition, wide 16:9 aspect ratio, deep purple and indigo gradient background, floating geometric wireframe shapes — icosahedrons and torus knots, soft volumetric lighting, minimalist tech aesthetic, clean negative space on the left --ar 16:9 --v 6

Result: Better composition. The negative space directive gives me room for text. Wireframe shapes add visual interest without clutter. Still feels a bit generic though.

Now I'm actually directing the tool. I've specified the aspect ratio, the colour palette (not just "purple" but "deep purple and indigo gradient"), the specific geometric shapes, and — critically — where the negative space should be. That last one is what separates a usable hero image from a decorative one.

Version 3 — Refining the style

v3 — Refining style

editorial hero banner, 16:9, deep purple #534AB7 and indigo gradient background with subtle grain texture, delicate white wireframe geometry — icosahedron, dodecahedron, torus — floating at varying depths, soft directional light from upper right, generous negative space left third, Swiss graphic design influence, restrained and sophisticated --ar 16:9 --v 6 --style raw

Result: Much closer. The hex colour anchor helps the model stay on palette. "Swiss graphic design influence" pulls the composition toward grid-based thinking. The grain texture request gives it that tactile quality I want.

This is where the real work happens. I'm no longer describing what I want — I'm describing how it should feel. "Restrained and sophisticated" is not a visual description, but Midjourney v6 actually responds to tonal language. The --style raw flag reduces the model's aesthetic smoothing, giving me more control over the output.

Version 4 — Dialling the parameters

v4 — Parameter tuning

editorial hero banner, 16:9, deep purple #534AB7 to near-black gradient background, subtle analog film grain, delicate white wireframe icosahedron and torus knots at varying opacity, soft key light upper right rim light on geometry, negative space left third for typography, Swiss graphic design, Bauhaus minimalism, restrained palette --ar 16:9 --v 6 --style raw --s 250 --chaos 15

Result: The stylize value of 250 (out of 1000) keeps the model from over-embellishing. Chaos at 15 introduces just enough variation between runs to give me options without going wild. This is the version I'd actually use as a starting point for post-production.

Version 5 — The polished prompt

v5 — Polished

editorial web hero, 16:9 composition, gradient from #534AB7 to #111110, subtle film grain overlay feel, sparse white wireframe geometry — single icosahedron centre-right, faint torus knot background left — rim lit from upper right, deep shadows, generous left negative space for headline text, Swiss International Style grid discipline, Bauhaus restraint, contemporary tech portfolio aesthetic, moody but professional --ar 16:9 --v 6 --style raw --s 150 --chaos 8

Result: This is the one. Single focal object (icosahedron), secondary background element (torus), clear lighting direction, specific negative space allocation, and stylistic anchors that pull in a consistent direction. Stylize at 150 gives the model almost no creative licence — I'm directing, not suggesting.

The system

My iteration pattern is consistent across every prompt I write:

  • v1: Brain dump — get the concept out, accept it will be bad
  • v2: Structure — add composition, aspect ratio, spatial directives
  • v3: Style — introduce aesthetic references, colour specificity, mood language
  • v4: Parameters — tune --stylize, --chaos, and --style raw for control
  • v5: Polish — reduce complexity, sharpen the focal point, tighten every word

The key insight: each version should change only one or two things. If you change five parameters at once, you don't know what caused the improvement (or the regression). Treat it like debugging — isolate variables, test, observe, iterate.

The outputs from v5 still need post-production in Photoshop. I adjust levels, clean up artefacts, and sometimes composite the best elements from multiple generations. AI generates the raw material; human direction makes it finished work.
